## PubChem Periodic Table (NCBI / US National Library of Medicine)

- Used for: names, symbols, electron configuration, electronegativity, atomic radius,
  ionization energy, electron affinity, oxidation states, standard state, melting and
  boiling points, density, group block (category), year discovered, CPK colour; and the
  atomic mass of elements with no NIST standard atomic weight (Am-Og).
- Licence: PubChem is a service of NCBI, a US-government agency; NCBI places no
  restrictions on the use or distribution of PubChem data
  (<https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/home/about/policies/>). The periodic-table values are
  basic physical facts compiled from standard references. Attribution to PubChem is
  courteous and is given here and in the README.
- Not used: PubChem's `AtomicWeight` column for elements NIST covers, because it mixes
  abridged and rounded values (Fe 55.84, Li 7.0, Pb 207); NIST and IUPAC values are used
  instead.

## NIST Atomic Weights and Isotopic Compositions (NIST PML)

- Used for: every isotope row (relative atomic mass with uncertainty and the `#`
  estimated flag, representative isotopic composition), the standard atomic weight text,
  and its `g`/`m`/`r` notes.
- Licence: NIST Standard Reference Data produced by the US government is not subject to
  copyright in the United States (17 U.S.C. §105); see
  <https://www.nist.gov/open/copyright-fair-use-and-licensing-statements-srd-data-software-and-technical-series-publications>.
  Cite as: Coursey, J.S., Schwab, D.J., Tsai, J.J., and Dragoset, R.A., *Atomic Weights and
  Isotopic Compositions*, NIST Physical Measurement Laboratory.
- Upstream quirks handled by the build script:
  - The file is HTML; values are in one `<pre>` block of `Key = value` records.
  - Hydrogen-2 and -3 are labelled `D` and `T`; elements 115 and 117 still carry the
    placeholder symbols `Uup` and `Uus`. The bundle uses the element's symbol (H, Mc, Ts)
    taken from PubChem by atomic number.
  - Missing values are `&nbsp;`, and C-12's mass is published as `12.0000000(00)`, so its
    uncertainty is 0.
  - The edition on NIST's site predates some IUPAC revisions (for example Ar 39.948,
    Pb 207.2, Yb 173.054, Zr 91.224 rather than the newer intervals or values). The bundle
    reports NIST as published.

## IUPAC CIAAW conventional atomic weights

- Used for: `atomic_mass` of the twelve elements whose standard atomic weight is an
  interval (H 1.008, Li 6.94, B 10.81, C 12.011, N 14.007, O 15.999, Mg 24.305, Si 28.085,
  S 32.06, Cl 35.45, Br 79.904, Tl 204.38), hard-coded in `tools/build-data.ts` as
  `CONVENTIONAL_ATOMIC_WEIGHTS`. Source: <https://www.ciaaw.org/atomic-weights.htm>.
- These are individual published scientific facts (twelve numbers), not a copied dataset.

## Derived values

`group_number`, `period` and `block` are computed from the atomic number (18-column IUPAC
layout; La-Lu and Ac-Lr have NULL group and block `f`). `atomic_mass_basis`,
`properties_predicted`, `discovered_in_antiquity`, `nuclide` and `is_most_abundant` are
derived by the build script. These are labelled as derived in the column comments.

Not used: Bowserinator's Periodic-Table-JSON (CC-BY-SA, share-alike).
